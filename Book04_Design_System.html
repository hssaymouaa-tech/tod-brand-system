For joy, culture, energy — stories, social, youth-led campaigns. Apply as a gradient bloom, a striped pattern, or a single accent chip — never as flat brand chrome, and never assigned to a content category. The palette is decorative; the primary pair (Yellow · Navy) always leads.

Purple stays separate

Joy Violet is warm and decorative — Happy Palette only. The Expression Accent purples are a cooler, distinct hue reserved for one job: signalling that a story carries the weight of the beIN lineage. The two purples are never interchangeable and never appear in the same composition.

Print production note

Coated (C) values are calibrated for gloss/silk stock; Uncoated (U) for matte/natural stock — they will look different on press, so specify the right one. For premium applications, TOD Yellow may also be reproduced as a metallic spot (PMS 871 C / gold) where the budget and finish allow. Always run a press proof before a large print run.

Digital Tokens · TOD Design System (TDS)

The product palette. Five ramps, ten steps each.

The brand palette above governs identity. For digital product — web, mobile, TV apps — the live TOD Design System (TDS) resolves colour into systematic 10-step token ramps. Each ramp's 500 step is its base; lighter steps (00–400) and darker steps (600–900) are derived for surfaces, hover, borders, and states. Source of truth: tds.ihabeeb.com/tokens/colors.

Brand ↔ Product reconciliation

The TDS Primary 500 is #FFBC00 — the screen-optimised interactive yellow used for CTAs, links, and active states in product. It is intentionally distinct from the brand-signal TOD Yellow #FFC72C, which remains the locked value for logo, marketing, broadcast, and print. Use #FFC72C for brand expression; use the TDS Primary ramp for interactive UI.

Accessibility · WCAG Contrast

Legible for everyone. Captions are not optional.

Every text-and-background pair must meet WCAG 2.1. Target AA (4.5:1) for body copy and UI, and AAA (7:1) for captions, subtitles, and small functional text — the legibility floor for an inclusive streaming experience.

Why this matters

Captions serve deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers, second-language audiences, and anyone watching with sound off. A solid 82% Navy box guarantees legibility over any video — bright pitch, dark arena, or busy crowd — and keeps every TOD caption at AAA, never dependent on the footage behind it.

4.5 · Grid & Layout

One spacing rhythm. Built on an 8-pt base.

Every margin, padding, and gap is a multiple of 8. Layouts use a 12-column grid on desktop, collapsing to 6 on tablet and 4 on mobile. Max content width is locked to keep long-form reading comfortable.

Conversion Cards

The contextual upsell shown in place of gated content — Kids hubs, anonymous Sport access, Live Sport blocks. One card, one slot, never stacked. Five parts, always in this order: eyebrow label, headline (sell the value, not the lock), one line of support copy, primary CTA, optional secondary action.

AnatomyPurpose
01 · EyebrowNames what's gated — "Kids", "Live Sport", "This match".
02 · HeadlineThe value on the other side of the gate. Never leads with "Locked" or "Unavailable".
03 · Support lineOne sentence, states the unlock condition in plain terms.
04 · Primary CTASingle action, brightest element on the card. .btn-cta, TOD Yellow.
05 · Secondary (optional)Dismiss / "not now" / explore-free-tier — always visually subordinate.

State variants

Same anatomy, different copy contract per access state. Layout never changes — only headline, support line, and CTA label.

State · Anonymous

Sign up

Registration framing. Headline sells what an account unlocks, not what's missing.

State · Freemium

Upgrade

Premium-feature framing. Headline names the specific premium benefit, not "Go Premium" alone.

State · Lapsed

Reactivate

Win-back framing. Acknowledge the return — never re-run new-user acquisition copy on a lapsed account.

Platform behaviour

Same five parts, different shell. The card never gains or loses anatomy by platform — only density.

Desktop

Inline or modal

Full padding, all five parts visible without interaction.

Tablet

Same card, tighter

Identical anatomy, reduced padding. No layout reflow between tablet and desktop.

Mobile

Bottom sheet

Primary CTA visible above the fold without scrolling; secondary action may collapse under a "more" affordance.

Hard rule

Primary CTA is always present and always the brightest element on the card — TOD Yellow .btn-cta, never the Happy Palette gradient. The gradient is decorative background only; it never carries a CTA fill. See 4.1 · Color & Accessibility for the full Happy Palette / Purple-separation rule.

4.7 · Imagery Direction

Three approved image worlds. Nothing else.

Every brand image belongs to one of three styles. Subjects are limited to our core worlds — football, tennis, basketball, F1, and entertainment genres. No stock clichés, no generic crowds.

01 · Architectural

Matte white-clay maquettes on light grey. Used for chapter dividers and brand-structural moments. Calm, premium, abstract.

02 · Sports Action

Cinematic, amber-lit, decisive moments. Football, tennis, basketball, F1 only. Energy and authority.

03 · Entertainment

Genre stills — drama, sci-fi, action, thriller. The "stories" half of No. 1 Sports & Stories. Cinematic, never campy.

Subject whitelist

Approved subjects: Football · Tennis · Basketball · F1 · Entertainment genres. Anything outside this set needs brand approval. Logos over imagery follow Book 02 clear-space and contrast rules.
